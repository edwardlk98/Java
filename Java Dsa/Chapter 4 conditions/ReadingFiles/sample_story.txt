The Last Train Home

The rain had been falling since sunset, turning the streets into shining rivers beneath the city lights. Marcus stood alone on the empty platform, watching drops slide down the glass shelter.

The station clock read 11:47 PM. The last train was supposed to arrive at midnight.

Marcus checked his phone. One message waited for him.

“Don’t forget what you promised.”

He read it twice before putting the phone away.

A distant rumble echoed through the tunnel, but instead of headlights, a faint blue light appeared between the tracks. Marcus stepped closer to the edge.

The light grew brighter.

Then the train arrived.

It looked almost exactly like the train he had taken every night for the past five years, except there were no advertisements on the walls and every window was dark.

The doors opened.

Marcus hesitated.

Inside, every seat was empty.

Except for one.

An old man sat at the far end of the train, holding a small wooden box on his lap.

“Are you getting on?” the man asked.

Marcus looked at the station clock.

11:59 PM.

He stepped inside.

The doors closed behind him.

As the train began to move, the old man smiled and placed the wooden box on the seat beside him.

“You've been looking for this for a long time,” he said.

Marcus stared at the box.

He recognized it immediately.

It was the same box his father had disappeared with twenty years ago.

For the first time that night, Marcus forgot about the message on his phone.

He reached for the box.

The train entered the darkness of the tunnel, and every light inside went out.

When they came back on, the old man was gone.

So was the train.

Marcus was standing alone on a platform he had never seen before.

The wooden box was still in his hands.

Slowly, he opened it.

Inside was a photograph of himself as a child, standing beside his father.

On the back, written in his father's handwriting, were six words:

“I knew you would come back.”

Marcus looked down the empty platform.

For the first time in twenty years, he felt that he was finally home.
